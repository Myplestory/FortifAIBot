# Dreyfus Model of Skill Acquisition — Cross-Domain Foundation

The Dreyfus model is the cross-discipline foundation for the band ladder used in this protocol. It applies to any structured skill domain (clinical medicine, nursing, engineering, ultrasound, aviation) and provides the mental-stage taxonomy that domain-specific frameworks (SWECOM, SFIA, and any equivalents you stitch in for other disciplines) map onto. Reference its verbatim stage definitions when justifying band scores in your `reason` and `citations` fields.

## Source: Dreyfus Model of Skill Acquisition

Stuart E. Dreyfus & Hubert L. Dreyfus, "A Five-Stage Model of the Mental Activities Involved in Directed Skill Acquisition," University of California, Berkeley, Operations Research Center, 1980. Report for the U.S. Air Force Office of Scientific Research (Contract F49620-79-C-0063). Revised in: Dreyfus & Rousse, "Revisiting the Six Stages of Skill Acquisition," in *Teaching and Learning for Adult Skill Acquisition: Applying the Dreyfus and Dreyfus Model in Different Fields*, 2021.

The Dreyfus model defines five stages of skill acquisition, each characterized by a transformation across four mental functions: recollection (non-situational → situational), recognition (decomposed → holistic), decision (analytical → intuitive), and awareness (monitoring → absorbed).

**Verbatim stage definitions** (synthesized from Dreyfus & Dreyfus 1980; Peña 2010, *Medical Education Online* 15:4846, PMC2887319; ASEE 2008 applied to engineering):

> **Novice:** "In the novice stage a person follows rules that are context-free and feels no responsibility for anything other than following the rules." Cannot exercise discretionary judgment. Rigid adherence to taught rules or plans. (Peña 2010, citing Dreyfus & Dreyfus 1980.)

> **Advanced Beginner:** "The advanced beginner is beginning to connect relevant contexts to the rules and facts they are learning. Adding to their knowledge of facts, they recognize previously undefined facts and increase their knowledge of relevant concepts, vocabulary, and principles. Individuals at this level may have no sense of practical priority." (CABEM, citing Dreyfus & Dreyfus 1980.)

> **Competent:** "Competence develops after having considerable experience." The competent performer "encounters situations where the rules don't apply or where it is unclear which rules should be used." They "begin to see their actions in the context of long-term goals through conscious and deliberate planning." "While more fluid than advanced beginners, competent performance still proceeds by analysis, calculation, and deliberate rule-following." Success and failure "partially depend on the performer's choice of perspective and not just on how well they follow rules. This leads to higher emotional involvement." (Wikipedia, citing Dreyfus & Dreyfus 1980/1986; FourWeekMBA, citing Dreyfus & Dreyfus 1980.)

> **Proficient:** "Proficiency is shown in individuals who use intuition in decision making and develop their own rules to formulate plans." "The proficient individual can select an appropriate course of action for any situation with confidence. This is the point where learning transitions from rule-based to situation-based." "Proficient performers intuitively grasp what a situation calls for but consciously decide responses." (Peña 2010; FourWeekMBA; Wikipedia, all citing Dreyfus & Dreyfus 1980/1986.)

> **Expert:** "Expertise is characterized by a fluid performance that happens unconsciously, automatically, and no longer depends on explicit knowledge." "In the expert stage, action is driven by intuition derived from a deep understanding of the subject matter." "The expert pilot, having finally reached this non-analytical stage of performance, responds intuitively and appropriately to his current situation." (Peña 2010, citing Dreyfus & Dreyfus 1980, p. 12; FourWeekMBA.)

**Mapping to band scores:**

| Dreyfus Stage | Verbatim Key Characteristic | Band |
|---|---|---|
| Novice | "follows rules that are context-free and feels no responsibility for anything other than following the rules" | Entry (score 1-2) |

**Empirical basis for the mapping:** Dreyfus & Dreyfus (1980) established through observation of Air Force pilots, chess players, and automobile drivers that skill acquisition follows this invariant progression regardless of domain. The model has been empirically validated across clinical medicine (Peña, 2010, *Medical Education Online* 15:4846), nursing (Benner, 1984, *From Novice to Expert*, Addison-Wesley), engineering education (ASEE 2008, "Dreyfus Five-Stage Model of Adult Skills Acquisition Applied to Engineering Lifelong Learning"), and ultrasound training (Tolsgaard et al., 2013, *Ultrasound in Obstetrics & Gynecology*, PMC4760538).

The key empirical claim: **competence (mid-level) is distinguished from proficiency/expertise (senior-level) by the shift from analytical to intuitive decision-making.** At mid-level, the practitioner "still proceeds by analysis, calculation, and deliberate rule-following." At senior-level, the practitioner's "learning transitions from rule-based to situation-based" and performance "no longer depends on explicit knowledge." This is why the senior grading criterion requires "commit to the right answer under pressure" — it tests for the proficient/expert stage's intuitive holistic recognition, not the competent stage's analytical decomposition.
